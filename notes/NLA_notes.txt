ABSTRACT:

Neuron level analysis to identify neurons that drive cultural behaviour.
Gradient-based scoring method with additional filtering.
Culture-general and culture-specific neurons account for < 1% of all neurons and concentrated in shallow to middle MLP layers.
Supressing them degrades performance on benchmarks by 30%, but perf. on general NLU benchmarks remains largely unaffected.

Culture-specific neurons support knowledge of both the target culture and related cultures.

Fine-tuning on NLU benchmarks diminishes LLMs cultural understanding.

Methods:

In transformers, two types of neurons are present -> MLP and attention.
This paper decomposes each type of neuron into subvalues.

for MLP neurons -> key-value decomposition.
Key -> when neuron should be activated.
Value -> what neuron adds to the hidden state when activated.

for Attention -> query, key, value vector decompositon.

P(y | x) -> probability of output y when input = x.

now for neuron, lth layer, kth neuron and ith token,
score(l, k, i)(x, y) = n(l, k, i) * DP(y | x)/Dn(l, k, i)

this signifies how active neuron is and how sensitive output probability is to that neuron.
so If neuron fires strongly and changing it highly changes answer prob -> its an important neuron.

this score is attribution score.
max score is taken for all tokens that is i from 1....n(n = no. of tokens)

Question: what if a neuron achieves max score on a token like "the", "hello", etc, basically -> stop words.
There can be another score for just stop words and then remove them too.

Taylor approximation formula, I havent quite understood it, but its a formula for how probability changes when neuron activation changes from average value to some other value.
So we can calculate score of a neuron in one pass efficiently.

Causal effect -> How much worse would answer get if the neuron is masked.

Aggregate formula -> For a dataset with Q questions, we aggregate the sum as some neurons are important throughtout the dataset.

MCQS from BLEnD is used. Blend is categorized into (food, work-life, sport) for neuron identification, and (education, family, holidays/celeberations) for eval (BLEnD[test]).
BLEnD = 500 questions.
this paper samples upto  5 instances per question, so ~12700 in BLENDnuer and ~10300 in test.

BLENDctrl I think is same as BLENDnuer but question content is removed. Scores = score(neur) - score(ctrl), so neurons with just task understanding are elmiminated.
CRC dataset is used (correct ans is always a country.)

for culture general,
top t% of score(neur) - score(ctrl) and then excluding top r% by score(CRC), so neurons activated due to countries are gone.

for culture specific,
score is calculated same way, then z-score of each nueron over 16 countries is calc, using standard dev and mean of score of a neuron across countries.
neurons with z-score < 0.5 are removed, as they are likely to contribute to culture just generally.

a - majority ans among all participants from country c, p = model prediction and max distance = max possible distance between options and a_of_c.
higher score = stronger alignment.

instance-level analysis of nueron scores

Authors propose a question,
"Do these neurons encode meta-level control signals for cultural contexts or knowledge-level
concepts?"
Basically means -> are these neurons deciding how to act based on the cultural context? or they just storing words/facts/training data?

Authors plot the scores of BlendNeur where model assigns scores > 0.5
Authors suggest if the neuron encodes meta-level control signals, it should have high scores on most instances.

in the figure, they plot scores of a single nueron of gemma per instance in BLEnD(neur). (instance = one question)

Neuron is active only 29% of instances, so a single neuron does not fire for all cultural questions.
This alone suggests to me that neurons do store some info/word, thye dont react to every cultural word, only some.

Same goes for culture specific neurons, the neuron activates for only 38% of Iran questions, but it also shows a high reaction for Azerbaijan, so that neuron stores something common to both Iran and Azerbaijan.

They also use NormADneur and test and mask nuerons using the same formulae, and neuron with high scores for BlendNeur score high on NormADtest even if they score less on NormADneur, so neurons do encode some general level facts and are not simply reacting to a particular word.

Implementation detail:
If they select an MLP gate projection, they also include its corresponding up/down projections.
If they select attention q/k/v modules, they include related q/k/v/out projections.

two types of modules are fine tuned:
1. Those that have the most culture general neurons.
2. Those that contain few/none culture general neurons.

Im guessing modules mean layers here.
Gemma is finetuned and only 10% params are updated.

QLNI and MRPC are NLU benchmarks.

So in training, performance for QLNI and MRPC increases.
But for cultural benchmarks, top modules suffer a large drop, as compared to the bottom modules.

Updating Top modules causes a larger drop on cultural benchmarks.
Updating Bottom modules preserves cultural ability better.

Interesting thing -> all models have a very similar number of culture-specific neurons. You would think that western or even chinese representation would be more but that isnt the case.
